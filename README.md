# Analyzing-UK-Social-Care-Notes
# NLP-Driven Decision Support for Electronic Care Notes in UK Domiciliary and Care Home Settings: A Synthetic-Data Feasibility Study of Escalation Detection.
The electronic care notes in the Digital Social Care Records (DSCRs) of the UK contain valuable information relating to safety and quality; however, the information is often contained within free text, which is hard to search, summarise and act upon when time is limited. This dissertation develops and tests a Natural Language Processing (NLP) clinical decision support (CDS) model for adult social care notes, which also encompasses escalation detection using a mixture of free-text signals and structured Minimum Operational Data Standard (MODS) features. With a design science approach and an augmented, aligned corpus of escalation, seven classifiers were tested and compared: TF-IDF logistic regression, LSTM network, GRU network, Bi-LSTM network, DistilBERT network, RoBERTa network, and ModernBERT network. DistilBERT and RoBERTa were more accurate (86.9% accuracy; F1 0.878) than the other classical and recurrent baselines on the held-out test set. No significant improvements were observed from hyperparameter search and hybrid ensembling. The negative finding is the poor performance of the extended pipeline in relation extraction (F1 0.025) and a rather low performance in entity extraction (F1 0.425). In general, the findings of the study confirm the feasibility of NLP-based care-note triage in the context of augmented data but are not ready for live DSCR deployment. Further work in the areas of real-service pilot, usability evaluation and improved relation modelling is still required.
